## 4. The finding that matters

**This participant declined the premise of the capstone.** Asked about working across
music services, he said it would be "nice," then said plainly that he *"doesn't mind not
being able to work with other music services."* He also said he *"cares more about the
music than where he listens,"* i.e. platform identity is not a thing he feels friction
about.

Consequences, reflected in `docs/backlog.md`:

- **J5 (send a playlist across services) has zero support from W01.** The bridge stories
  S7–S9 sit in a holding pen and are marked provisional in MUST.
- **S10 (collaborative cross-service playlist) is treated as unevidenced**, despite the
  "nice to" quote, because the same participant contradicted it in the same interview.
- **S1/S2 are the best-evidenced stories in the backlog and sit in COULD**, because they
  serve a job the current product does not do.

This is one interview with one person who is not the target user for cross-service
sharing. It is not sufficient to kill or confirm the capstone. W02 and W03 must be run
with people who have actually had a playlist stranded on the wrong service.

## 6. The one request that must be refused

*"Maybe having downloaded songs and someother app allows you to upload them / sync music
apps together to play songs together"*

Downloaded Spotify and Apple Music files are DRM-encrypted and licensed to the account,
not the listener. Audio cannot be moved between services, by this product or any other.
This is in the backlog's WON'T column with that reason. Worth noting that the
participant's own best idea is the one thing that is legally unbuildable — a useful thing
to say out loud when presenting.
